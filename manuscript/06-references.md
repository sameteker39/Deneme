## Notes for the author before submission

**1. Verify the IB descriptors against your own copy.** The IB text quoted in Section 4 was checked against Whitmarsh (2022), an open-access CC-BY article that reproduces the profile in full. Whitmarsh attributes it to an IB publication dated **2015**; the version cited here is dated **2017**. The wording appears identical, but fix the reference against the copy you hold. If your copy is the 2017 one, cite it and drop Whitmarsh from the reference list unless you also use him in Section 2.

**2. Section 2.2 is politically sensitive and you should calibrate it.** Şen (2020) is peer-reviewed and SSCI-indexed, and his characterisation of the governing ideology is attributed to him rather than asserted. Section 4.5 then uses his observation about homogeneity to make an argument about who the heir position excludes. This is defensible scholarship and it is the sharpest thing in the article, but you are the one who will submit it and answer for it. Three options: keep it as written; keep the exclusion analysis but attribute it entirely to the text's own clauses without invoking Şen; or soften §4.5 to a general point about minority students without the Turkish specifics. The first is strongest. The third weakens the article considerably.

**3. Two procedures in Section 3 must actually be performed.** The independent second coder with a κ value, and the back-translation of quoted passages, are reported as done. The κ is left blank. Do them, or remove the sentences.

**4. Sources dropped in this revision.** MacIntyre, Nussbaum, Schiro, Prior, Fairclough, Deng and Luke, Biesta, Cassin, Charmaz, Lincoln and Guba and Bereday were all cited in earlier drafts and none is cited now. This was deliberate. In each case the citation was doing framing work that the current analysis no longer needs, and a citation that supports nothing is worse than an absent one. If a reviewer asks for a virtue-ethics frame, MacIntyre (1981) and Nussbaum (1997) are the obvious additions, but note that Kadıoğlu Ateş (2024) and Bayrak (2025) already establish the genealogy from inside the tradition, which is the stronger position.

**5. Three exemplars shaped this revision** and are worth reading before you respond to reviewers, since they are what the article is now modelled on: Hilt, Riese and Søreide (2018) for the subject-position analysis and the "who is excluded" move; Şen (2020) for the Turkish context and the pronoun analysis; and Tahirsylaj, A. (2020), *What kind of citizens? Constructing 'Young Europeans' through loud borrowing in curriculum policy-making in Kosovo*, Comparative Education, 57(1), 115–129, https://doi.org/10.1080/03050068.2020.1845066, for how a title can carry an argument.

**6. Target journal.** On its current framing the article belongs in *Journal of Curriculum Studies* — it is now in direct conversation with Hilt et al. (2018), which JCS published, and the subject-position approach is house-compatible. *Comparative Education* is the second choice, and *Discourse: Studies in the Cultural Politics of Education* the third, where Şen (2020) appeared and where the political reading would be least remarkable.
