# 2. Conceptual Framework

The analysis draws on four bodies of work: an account of what learner profiles are as documents, a vocabulary for describing educational purpose that does not belong to either system, TYMM's own conceptual apparatus, and a set of warnings about how comparisons in this field go wrong. They are set out in that order because the last constrains how the first three may be used.

## 2.1 Profiles as documents that act

Learner profiles are not descriptions. No system claims that its graduates already possess the qualities its profile names, and no profile is falsified by a graduate who lacks them. They are prescriptive statements that acquire force through circulation, and their function is closer to that of a constitution's preamble than to that of a specification.

Prior's (2003) treatment of documents as agents rather than containers is the relevant starting point. A document does things: it authorises, it licenses, it forecloses. A learner profile authorises certain conversations about what schools are for and makes others harder to have, and it does this by fixing a vocabulary that subsequent documents must use. In TYMM's case the mechanism is explicit, since the profile characteristics reappear as the stated foundation of the Kapsamlı Gelişimsel Rehberlik Programı and connect downward through the values framework into learning outcomes. In the IB's case the mechanism is looser and, according to Tocci, Ryan and Ensminger (2021), correspondingly variable in effect.

Apple (2004) supplies the political reading. Curriculum encodes decisions about which knowledge and whose identities are legitimate, and those decisions are usually distributed across a document in ways that require excavation. The profile is the exception: it is the place where the decisions are stated outright, in a form short enough that inclusions and omissions are both visible. Schiro's (2008) mapping of curriculum ideologies is used here in the same spirit, as an argument that the person imagined at the end of schooling is the most economical way to characterise what a curriculum is committed to.

This is why the study treats what a profile declines to name as evidence. In a document of this length, omission is not oversight.

## 2.2 A vocabulary neutral between the two systems

Comparing two profiles requires terms that belong to neither. Biesta's (2010) account of educational purpose supplies them. He distinguishes three functions that education always performs simultaneously: qualification, the transmission of knowledge and skill; socialisation, the induction of the learner into existing orders and traditions; and subjectification, the emergence of the learner as a subject capable of acting on their own account.

The value of the distinction here is that neither system's self-description maps neatly onto it, which is what makes it usable. Both profiles can be read as covering all three functions, and the analytic question is not which function a profile serves but how it distributes weight and, more sharply, how it construes the relation between socialisation and subjectification. Biesta's own argument is that subjectification is the function most easily lost, because qualification and socialisation are the two that institutions can measure and defend. A profile that specifies the tradition into which the learner is inducted, and specifies it in detail, invites the question of what remains for subjectification; a profile that specifies no tradition at all invites the question of what the emergent subject is emerging into. The framework is applied in that two-sided way rather than as a test one profile passes.

## 2.3 TYMM's own apparatus as the first coding lens

TYMM does not arrive without a theory of itself. The Ortak Metni organises the profile around four constructs, and the study takes them as its first-cycle codes.

*Ontolojik bütünlük* (ontological wholeness) treats the person as *ruh* and *beden*, spirit and body, where *ruh* is defined as the sum of "entelektüel, ahlaki ve duygusal" faculties: intellectual, moral and emotional. The text further specifies that self-governance depends on "akıl ve vicdanın birlikte hareket etmesi", reason and conscience acting together. This is a substantive anthropological claim, not a preamble, and it has consequences the findings trace.

*Epistemolojik bütünlük* (epistemological wholeness) concerns how the learner reaches and uses knowledge, and terminates in *bilgelik*, wisdom, which the text defines as the maturity arising when "bilgi ve erdem" (knowledge and virtue) come together. Knowledge that has not been joined to virtue is, in this construal, incomplete rather than merely partial.

*Zamansal bütünlük* (temporal wholeness) extends the learner's relevant past beyond their biography to the shared history of the society they belong to, and specifies that this is achieved by transmitting "milletçe sahip olunan ortak bilinç, kültür ve değerler".

*Aksiyolojik olgunluk* (axiological maturity) is described as the unifying and completing dimension of the profile, and as the foundation of the Erdem-Değer-Eylem Çerçevesi. It concerns the maturing of ethical and aesthetic judgement together.

Using these as the initial codebook is a deliberate methodological commitment rather than a courtesy. It means the analysis begins by asking what TYMM is doing on its own terms, and only then asks what the IB profile looks like from that vantage, the reverse of the usual direction. The choice has costs, and §3.4 states them.

## 2.4 Virtue as a live category, not a period label

*Erdem* is the term TYMM places at the centre of its profile, and there is an established temptation to file it under virtue ethics and treat the filing as an analysis. MacIntyre's (1981) argument that modern moral discourse retains the fragments of an older tradition without its coherence, and Nussbaum's (1997) case for cultivating capacities rather than transmitting doctrine, are both relevant, and both are used here with a restriction: as points of comparison for what TYMM's text actually says, not as an explanation of where it comes from.

The restriction matters because the genealogical work has already been done, and done from inside the tradition. Kadıoğlu Ateş (2024) traces the competent and virtuous person through moral philosophy from the Sophists onward. Bayrak (2025) reads the profile's characteristics against the *Kutadgu Bilig*, finding substantial overlap and a clear difference: where the eleventh-century text addresses rulers and grounds its counsel traditionally and religiously, TYMM addresses every learner and frames development in contemporary terms. Attaching a Western label to a construct whose lineage has been documented locally would add nothing and would perform precisely the substitution Takayama, Sriprakash and Connell (2017) warn against.

What virtue ethics does contribute is a question rather than a classification. If the object of education is a state of the person rather than a set of capacities, then education cannot be fully described by what a graduate can do, and any profile committed to such an object will have to name qualities that are not performances. Whether the IB profile names any such qualities, and whether TYMM's are of that kind, is a matter for the findings.

## 2.5 Constraints on the comparison

Two arguments limit what the study may claim.

Takayama, Sriprakash and Connell (2017) identify the pattern in which comparative education renders non-Western cases legible only as departures from an unmarked norm, with the field's categories, journals and gatekeepers concentrated in the Anglophone world. The risk in this study is concrete: with a widely known transnational profile on one side and a national profile new to the literature on the other, the default is to describe TYMM in terms of how it differs from the IB. Three measures respond to it. The first coding cycle uses TYMM's constructs (§2.3). The findings report qualities absent from each profile symmetrically rather than treating absence from TYMM as a lack. And where a feature of TYMM admits both a reading internal to its framework and a reading from outside, both are stated.

Manzon (2011) supplies the second constraint. Units of comparison in this field are constructed, and the construction has to be argued. Placing a national framework beside a transnational one is not self-evidently legitimate, and the justification cannot be that both are called learner profiles. The study's justification is structural: both texts define the educated person through exactly ten named qualities elaborated in short passages. It is a claim about the texts' form, not about equivalent institutional weight. Where the analysis compares definitions rather than labels, the difference in specification between the two profiles is reported as a finding rather than corrected for.

Deng and Luke's (2008) work on how school subjects are defined and theorised is relevant to a third limit. Their argument that the constitution of curricular categories is itself a theoretical act applies to profile characteristics as much as to subjects: *bilge* and *knowledgeable* are not two labels for one underlying category but two different constructions of what an epistemic ideal consists of. The study treats correspondences in its mapping table as structural adjacencies and says so, rather than presenting them as translations.

## 2.6 Summary of the framework

The profile is read as a document that acts, in which omission is evidence. Biesta's three functions supply a vocabulary belonging to neither system. TYMM's four constructs supply the first-cycle codes, so that the national text sets the initial terms. Virtue ethics supplies a question about whether education aims at states or at performances, not a classification of TYMM. And two constraints govern the whole: the comparison must not be built so that one profile is the norm, and the unit of comparison must be defended as constructed rather than assumed.
