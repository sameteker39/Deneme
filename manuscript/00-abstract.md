# National and Global Visions of the Educated Person: A Textual Comparison of the Türkiye Yüzyılı Maarif Modeli and IB Learner Profiles

## Abstract

Learner profiles are the shortest and most exposed part of a curriculum: the place where a system states, in a page, what it takes schooling to be for. This article compares two of them, the learner profile of Türkiye's Türkiye Yüzyılı Maarif Modeli (TYMM), in force since September 2024, and the International Baccalaureate (IB) learner profile, through a qualitative comparative document analysis. The two texts share a genre and a structure: each defines the educated person through exactly ten named qualities elaborated in short descriptive passages. That structural symmetry is the study's tertium comparationis, and to avoid rendering the national framework legible only as a departure from the transnational one, the first coding cycle was built from TYMM's own constructs of ontological, epistemological and temporal wholeness and axiological maturity rather than from imported categories.

The analysis finds that the two profiles differ more sharply in how they are written than in what they contain. Their inventories of qualities correspond at seven of ten positions. Their grammar does not correspond at all: all ten IB descriptors are written in the first person plural, and all sixty-one of TYMM's component definitions in the third person aorist. TYMM names the collective the learner is answerable to (flag, language, homeland, nation, state), while the IB names no polity, language or tradition anywhere, requiring more than one language while specifying none. Most consequentially, TYMM attaches a clause of conformity to its grants of autonomy, so that independent thought proceeds "in a manner consistent with one's own values and beliefs" and aesthetic judgement is formed "in a manner consistent with moral values"; the IB attaches no such clause and includes the learner's own culture among what is to be critically appreciated. Six qualities have no counterpart across the pair: patriotism, aesthetics, will and productivity on one side, unconditioned open-mindedness and reflection on the other. Balance is struck on different axes, moral in one profile and physical in the other, and spiritual health is constitutive of the person in one and unmentioned in the other.

These patterns are not two positions on a single local-to-global axis. TYMM specifies sixty-one components because it governs one system with a known destination; the IB specifies none because it must function across schools in more than 160 countries. The two documents give incompatible answers to a real question, whether formation has a fixed point, and that incompatibility is not dissolved by proposing a hybrid, since specification is possible only where the collective is named and openness is possible only where it is not. The article argues that comparative work on learner profiles has concentrated on the part of the genre most likely to converge, and that the formal properties of these texts (grammatical person, named collectives, qualifying clauses) carry the argument about the person more reliably than any inventory of values does.

**Keywords:** learner profiles; comparative curriculum analysis; Türkiye Yüzyılı Maarif Modeli; International Baccalaureate; curriculum policy texts; virtue and formation; document analysis
