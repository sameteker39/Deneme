# Whose 'we'? How Türkiye's Maarif Model and the International Baccalaureate position the student

## Abstract

Learner profiles are the shortest part of a curriculum and the part that says most plainly what a system wants its graduates to become. In September 2024 Türkiye brought into force the Türkiye Yüzyılı Maarif Modeli, a national framework built around a stated end — raising *yetkin ve erdemli insanlar*, competent and virtuous people — and specified through ten profile characteristics. The International Baccalaureate has been describing its ideal learner through ten attributes for three decades. We compare the two texts and ask what position each makes available to the student, and who could not occupy it.

The two lists overlap substantially, and reading them for their contents would suggest two systems that want much the same thing with different accents. Reading them for how they address the student suggests otherwise. The IB profile speaks in the first person plural throughout: its learner is already inside a 'we' that the text never locates. Türkiye's profile speaks in the third person throughout: its learner is a figure described from outside, and the collective they answer to is named repeatedly and precisely — flag, language, homeland, nation, state. Where the Maarif Model grants independence of thought or aesthetic judgement, it attaches a clause requiring that what is granted stay consistent with commitments the learner already holds. The IB attaches no such clause, and asks its learner to appraise their own culture critically alongside everyone else's.

We read these as two positions rather than two value sets: an heir, entrusted with something they are expected not to lose, and a joiner, admitted to a community with no address. Each excludes someone. The heir position has no room for a student whose existing commitments are not the ones the text assumes; the joiner position has no room for a student whose tradition is not open to revision. We argue that comparative research on learner profiles has concentrated on their contents, which is the part of the genre most likely to converge, and that what these documents do to the student is done first through how they address them.

**Keywords:** learner profiles; subject positions; curriculum policy texts; Türkiye Yüzyılı Maarif Modeli; International Baccalaureate; comparative curriculum analysis
