# 5. Discussion

## 5.1 Form carries the argument

The finding that organises the rest is that the two profiles differ more legibly in how they are written than in what they contain. Their inventories of qualities overlap at seven of ten positions. Their grammar does not overlap at all: every IB descriptor is first person plural and every one of TYMM's sixty-one component definitions is third person aorist.

This has a consequence for how learner profiles should be read. The standard approach, lining the two lists up, noting which values appear on each side and inferring a difference in commitments, would have produced a modest and partly misleading result here. It would have reported that both profiles value inquiry, integrity and compassion, that TYMM adds patriotism and aesthetics, and that the IB adds open-mindedness and reflection. That is true as far as it goes, and it misses that the two documents position the learner differently before either says anything about values. The IB profile speaks as a "we" the learner is invited into. TYMM's profile speaks about a person the system intends to form. A reader who only counts values will not see this, because it is not in the values.

The claim is not that content comparison is worthless. It is that the profile is a genre with a small number of formal choices (person, voice, whether collectives are named, whether grants are qualified), and those choices carry the anthropological argument more reliably than the word list does. Apple's (2004) point that curriculum encodes decisions about legitimacy holds; what the analysis adds is that in this genre the encoding is often syntactic.

## 5.2 The proviso and the problem of subjectification

The pattern reported in §4.4 is the study's sharpest result and the one that most needs careful handling.

TYMM attaches a conformity clause to its grants of autonomy. Independent thought is exercised "in a manner consistent with their own values and beliefs"; aesthetic judgement is formed "in a manner consistent with moral values"; intercultural contact proceeds while "preserving their national identity and culture". The IB attaches nothing comparable, and its open-minded descriptor explicitly includes the learner's own culture in what is critically appreciated rather than exempting it.

Read through Biesta's (2010) three functions, this looks at first like a difference in the balance between socialisation and subjectification, with TYMM weighting the former. That reading is available and it is too quick. Biesta's own worry is that subjectification is squeezed out by the two functions institutions can measure, and neither profile is obviously guilty of that: TYMM's *irade* (will) and *bağımsız ve özgür düşünen* are subjectification claims, and the IB's attributes are as easily read as socialisation into a particular transnational disposition, which is close to Bunnell's (2010) and Resnik's (2012) institutional readings of the IB, and to Wells's (2011) observation that the profile asserts values without a theory of how they are acquired.

The more defensible formulation is that the two profiles disagree about whether formation has a fixed point. TYMM's answer is that it does, and its own construct of *aksiyolojik olgunluk* makes this a positive claim rather than a limitation: judgement that is not anchored is not, in that framework, mature. The IB's answer is that it does not, and that the encounter with difference should be able to reach anything, including the learner's starting commitments. These are genuine alternatives in moral education, not a spectrum with more freedom at one end.

Stating it this way matters for a reason beyond accuracy. A study that reported TYMM's provisos as constraints on an otherwise-granted freedom would have imported the IB's answer as the measure, the move Takayama, Sriprakash and Connell (2017) identify. A study that reported them only as *aksiyolojik olgunluk* would have accepted a framework's self-description as its analysis. Both readings are reported in §4.4 because the text sustains both, and the disagreement between them is the finding.

## 5.3 Specification and portability are traded against each other

TYMM specifies sixty-one components beneath ten characteristics; the IB specifies none. The temptation is to read this as thoroughness against vagueness. The institutional facts suggest a trade-off instead.

TYMM governs one system, in one language, under one ministry, and its profile is wired into a values framework and a skills framework that terminate in learning outcomes. Specification is possible because the destination is known. The IB profile has to function across roughly 5,900 schools in more than 160 countries, whose only shared feature is the IB itself. Tocci, Ryan and Ensminger (2021) show what its generality costs: teachers in one district implementing the profile in materially different ways, with the variation traceable to the breadth of the underlying concept. They also show what it buys, since a profile specified at TYMM's granularity would be unadoptable in most of those settings. Metlí and Lane (2020) report the same instability in international-mindedness itself, the construct the profile is meant to express.

So the two documents are not better and worse attempts at the same task. They are solutions to different constraint sets, and each pays for what it gains. This bears directly on the "hybrid model" that comparative work in this area tends to propose. Combining TYMM's moral specification with the IB's openness is not a synthesis available for the choosing: the specification is possible because the collective is named, and the openness is possible because it is not. A profile cannot both name the tradition the learner must preserve and leave the outcome of encounter genuinely open. Recommending that systems do both mistakes a trade-off for an oversight.

## 5.4 What the local–global frame obscures

Both texts resist the frame the comparison invites. TYMM is not inward-facing in any simple sense: *ahlaklı* requires respect for different beliefs and for other living beings, and *dostluk* includes building bonds with other cultures and taking part in international projects. The IB is not placeless: an unnamed universal is a position, and its multilingual requirement, its "global community", and its silence on any particular tradition constitute a stance rather than the absence of one.

The difference the evidence actually supports is about naming. TYMM names the object of obligation, in detail, seven components deep under *vatansever*. The IB names none. Naming is not the same as narrowness, and declining to name is not the same as openness. A profile that names its collective can be argued with, because what it asks for is on the page; a profile that does not name one is harder to contest and correspondingly harder to hold to account. Deng and Luke's (2008) argument that the constitution of curricular categories is a theoretical act applies here: whether a profile names its "we" is not a presentational detail but a decision about what kind of claim the profile is making.

## 5.5 Implications for practice

**For those writing learner profiles.** The formal choices are choices, and they are usually made without deliberation. Whether the profile speaks as "we" or describes "the student", whether the collective is named, and whether grants of autonomy carry provisos will do more to determine how the document is read than the selection of qualities will. Drafting groups typically spend their time on the list.

**For teacher education.** In TYMM settings, the sixty-one components are an unusual resource and an unusual demand: they specify what *ahlaklı* consists of to a degree that removes ambiguity but also leaves little room for professional interpretation. In IB settings the problem is inverted, and Tocci, Ryan and Ensminger (2021) document its effects. Neither condition is addressed by more familiarity with the profile text; both require attention to what the profile's level of specification leaves to the teacher.

**For policy.** Where systems adopt a transnational profile alongside a national one, the two will not conflict at the level of listed values, since the overlap is substantial. They will conflict on the proviso: a national profile that conditions openness on preserving identity and a transnational profile that conditions nothing are giving incompatible instructions about the same encounter. That is where the work of reconciliation, if it is attempted, has to be done.

## 5.6 Limitations

The study analyses intended curriculum only. It says nothing about what happens in classrooms, and no inference about teaching or learning under either framework follows from it. Ball's (1994) distinction between policy as text and policy as discourse marks the boundary: the analysis reaches the first and only the edge of the second.

The comparison is asymmetrical in ways that cannot be corrected, only declared. One profile governs a system and the other travels between them; one was analysed in the researcher's first language and the other in a second; one has three decades of interpretive apparatus around it and the other has been in force for two years.

The 2025 edition of the Ortak Metni is the version analysed. TYMM is recent and under revision, and the findings are indexed to that text.

The identification of a "conformity clause" pattern rests on three instances in the profile and its values framework. Three is enough to establish that the construction occurs and to show what it does. It is not enough to establish a rate, and no claim about frequency is made.

Finally, the ten-by-ten symmetry that makes the comparison tractable is a property of how the two documents present themselves. It licenses comparison of structural position, not the inference that a TYMM characteristic and an IB attribute do the same work, which is why Table 3 reports adjacencies rather than equivalences.

## 5.7 Directions for further work

Four lines follow directly.

The formal properties identified here are testable across a wider corpus. Whether grammatical person, named collectives and conformity clauses distinguish national from transnational profiles generally, or whether TYMM and the IB happen to sit at the extremes, requires a sample rather than a pair.

The enactment question is open and is the obvious complement. What teachers do with sixty-one specified components, and whether that specification narrows or supports professional judgement, cannot be answered from the text.

The revision history of both profiles is available and unexploited. The IB's shift from third person to first is documented; whether TYMM's provisos were present in the 2024 edition and how they have changed since would show what is settled in the framework and what is still moving.

And the study's own procedure, coding a transnational text through a national framework's constructs, is a method claim that has been demonstrated once. Whether it travels, and what it costs when the national framework's self-description is thinner than TYMM's, is worth establishing.

# 6. Conclusion

Learner profiles are the shortest and most exposed part of a curriculum, and the two examined here look alike. Each names ten qualities of an educated person and each is offered as the commitment organising everything else. Read at word level, they turn out to be doing different things, and the difference registers in grammar before it registers in content.

The IB profile is written in the first person plural and names no polity, language or tradition; the learner is addressed as already inside a collective that is never specified. TYMM's profile is written in the third person and names its collective repeatedly and precisely; the learner is described as a type the system intends to form, answerable to a flag, a language, a nation and a state. Where TYMM grants independence of thought or judgement it attaches a clause requiring consistency with values already held. The IB attaches nothing, and includes the learner's own culture among the things to be critically appreciated.

Neither pattern is a deficiency in the other's terms. TYMM's provisos follow from *aksiyolojik olgunluk*, its own construct, in which unanchored judgement is not mature judgement. The IB's silence about any particular collective is what allows one document to serve schools in 160 countries. What the comparison establishes is that these are incompatible answers to a real question, whether formation has a fixed point, and that the incompatibility cannot be dissolved by proposing a blend.

The wider claim is methodological. Comparative work on learner profiles has largely compared their contents, and their contents are the part most likely to converge, because the genre's conventions push every system toward naming inquiry, integrity and care. What does not converge is the form: who speaks, who is named, and what is held constant when the learner meets something unfamiliar. Reading for those properties makes visible a set of commitments that a value inventory leaves on the page unread.
