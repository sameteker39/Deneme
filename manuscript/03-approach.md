# 3. Approach

We read four documents. The core texts are the section of the Maarif Model's *Öğretim Programları Ortak Metni* (MEB, 2025) headed *Öğrenci Profili: Yetkin ve Erdemli İnsan*, and the IB learner profile (IBO, 2017). Around each we read a contextual text: the model's Erdem-Değer-Eylem tables, which set out its values framework, and *What is an IB education?*, which states the reasoning behind the attributes. The contextual texts informed our reading and supplied corroboration; the coded material came from the two core texts.

The Maarif Model has appeared in more than one edition since its approval. We analyse the 2025 edition and page references are to it. The IB profile has been revised repeatedly since the 1990s; we analyse the 2017 version, and where its wording departs from earlier versions in ways that bear on the analysis we treat the change as evidence rather than as noise.

What makes the two texts comparable is not their institutional weight, which is not comparable at all. It is that both belong to the same small genre and take the same form: a list of exactly ten named qualities, each elaborated in a short passage, presented as the point of everything else in the curriculum. That formal correspondence is our basis for setting them side by side, and it licenses comparison of structural position rather than the inference that a Turkish characteristic and an IB attribute do equivalent work.

Our reading was guided by the idea that policy texts make positions available. A document of this kind does not only describe a student; it offers a place from which a student can be recognised, and it does so through the ordinary machinery of language: who is addressed, who is spoken about, what is named, what is left general, what is granted and under what conditions (Weedon, 1997; Søreide, 2007; Hilt et al., 2018). This is why our findings turn on features that a thematic comparison would pass over. Şen's (2020) analysis of Turkish reform discourse proceeds similarly, tracking pronoun use to establish how a text situates itself in relation to those it speaks to.

We began with the Maarif Model's own vocabulary rather than with categories imported from elsewhere. Its four constructs of ontological, epistemological and temporal wholeness and axiological maturity organised the first reading of both texts. Applying a Turkish framework to an English document is not a neutral procedure and we do not present it as one; it makes some things visible and others less so. We chose it because the alternative, reading the Turkish text through the IB's categories, would have built the conclusion into the design in the way Takayama, Sriprakash and Connell (2017) describe.

A second reading was open. We re-read each quality and its elaboration for whatever the first reading had not caught, and what emerged was mostly grammatical: the person of the verbs, whether a collective was named or left indefinite, what kind of clause followed a grant of freedom. These became the categories reported in Section 4. We then arrayed the twenty qualities against them and read in both directions, down each profile to see how a feature distributed within it and across the pair to see where the two met, diverged, or had nothing to say to one another. Empty cells were treated as findings. In a document of this length, what is left out has been left out.

Because the core texts are in different languages, translation was a site of risk rather than a preliminary chore. All analysis of the Turkish text was done on the Turkish. Turkish terms stayed in Turkish in our working notes and English glosses were attached only for reporting. Several terms (*erdem*, *maarif*, *yetkinlik*, *irade*) have no single English equivalent, and we give an extended gloss at first use and keep the Turkish thereafter. Every passage quoted in Section 4 was independently back-translated by a bilingual colleague not otherwise involved, and discrepancies were reconciled before the gloss was fixed. Both texts were coded independently by a second reader fluent in Turkish and English; agreement on the initial categories was [κ = __], and disagreements were resolved in discussion, with categories revised where discussion showed them to be ambiguous rather than misapplied.

One of us was educated and works within the Turkish system. That gives access to a semantic field that is not recoverable from a dictionary: what *maarif* carries which *eğitim* does not, what *erdem* asks of a person. It also carries an obvious risk of reading one text generously and the other as the unmarked case. Our second reader was asked specifically to flag passages where the IB profile was being handled more thinly than the Maarif Model, and those passages were rewritten.

The study analyses documents. It has nothing to say about Turkish or IB classrooms, and no claim below should be read as evidence about teaching or learning under either framework. What a curriculum states and what schools do are different objects, and only the first is in view here.
