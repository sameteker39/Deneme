# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 A short genre with a large job

Curriculum documents are long and learner profiles are short. A national framework runs to hundreds of pages of outcomes, content maps and assessment guidance; the profile that opens it names ten or so qualities and fits on a single page. The brevity is the point. Where the rest of the document specifies what a system will teach, the profile states what the system is for, and it does so in a form compact enough to be reproduced on classroom walls, in parent handbooks and in ministerial speeches.

That compactness makes the profile the most exposed part of a curriculum. Everything a system is prepared to say publicly about the kind of person it intends to produce has to fit inside it, and everything it is not prepared to say has to be left out. Apple's (2004) argument that curricula encode decisions about whose knowledge counts applies with unusual directness here, because the profile is where those decisions stop being implicit. Schiro's (2008) treatment of curriculum ideologies makes the same point from the other side: competing visions of schooling differ less in their techniques than in the person they imagine at the end of them, and the profile is where that person is described.

Two developments make the genre worth examining now. Profiles of this kind have spread widely enough that the format itself has become conventional, so that systems with very different commitments produce documents that look alike. And a substantial new instance has just entered the field.

## 1.2 Two profiles that share a format

The International Baccalaureate learner profile is the reference case. Ten attributes — inquirers, knowledgeable, thinkers, communicators, principled, open-minded, caring, risk-takers, balanced, reflective — run across all four IB programmes and function, in Wells's (2011) phrase, as the thread that ties them together. The profile has been revised several times since its introduction, and the current version dates from 2017.

The Türkiye Yüzyılı Maarif Modeli (TYMM, Türkiye Century Education Model) is the new case. Approved by Türkiye's Board of Education on 23 May 2024 and brought into force on 9 September 2024, it replaced a curriculum architecture that had been in place for years and reorganised the whole system around a stated end: raising *yetkin ve erdemli insanlar*, competent and virtuous people. That end is specified through ten profile characteristics — ahlaklı, bilge, cesaretli, estetik, iradeli, merhametli, sağlıklı, sorgulayıcı, üretken, vatansever — distributed across two domains of wholeness, competence and virtue.

Ten and ten. Both sets are presented as the organising commitment of an entire curriculum, both are elaborated through short descriptive passages, and both mix cognitive with ethical qualities rather than separating them. A reader encountering the two lists side by side would reasonably expect the comparison to be a matter of matching items and noting where the values differ.

That expectation is what this study tests, and the test does not go the way the format suggests.

## 1.3 What the literature has and has not established

Research on the IB learner profile is not thin. Wells (2011) provides the standard critical analysis, observing that the profile asserts values while saying little about how values are acquired. Hill (2012) traces the longer history of the international-mindedness that the profile is meant to express, and Metlí and Lane (2020) show how unsettled that construct remains. Tocci, Ryan and Ensminger (2021) document what happens when teachers try to enact the profile in classrooms, finding wide variation traceable to the breadth of the underlying concept, and noting the particular difficulty it poses for those working from non-Western traditions. Bunnell (2010) and Resnik (2012) situate the IB institutionally rather than pedagogically, reading it as a system serving a mobile constituency.

What this literature has not had is a counterpart to compare against. The IB profile is usually analysed alone, or against other transnational frameworks, or against practitioner interpretations of itself. Where national systems enter, they typically enter as sites of IB implementation rather than as authors of competing profiles of their own.

TYMM is too recent for an English-language literature to have formed. The Turkish-language scholarship that exists has moved quickly and in a particular direction: Kadıoğlu Ateş (2024) places the competent and virtuous person within a lineage of moral philosophy running from classical Greece to Kohlberg, and Bayrak (2025) compares the profile's characteristics against the *Kutadgu Bilig*, an eleventh-century Turkish text of statecraft and ethics. Karaoğulları (2026) applies the ten characteristics as an analytic instrument to children's literature. These studies establish the profile's genealogy and demonstrate its usability. None compares it with a framework from outside the tradition it is drawn from.

The gap is therefore specific rather than general. It is not that learner profiles are understudied, nor that comparative curriculum work is scarce. It is that the two most consequential moves in the field, reading the IB profile critically and reading TYMM in its own intellectual lineage, have been made separately, and the comparison that would put pressure on both has not been attempted.

There is a reason to be careful about how such a comparison is set up. Takayama, Sriprakash and Connell (2017) describe a recurring failure in comparative and international education, in which a non-Western case is rendered intelligible only as a deviation from an unmarked Western norm, so that the analytic categories arrive already carrying the answer. A study that coded TYMM using the IB profile's own conceptual vocabulary would reproduce exactly this. Manzon (2011) makes the corresponding methodological point: the units of comparison in this field are constructed rather than discovered, and the construction is an argument that has to be defended rather than a preliminary that can be assumed.

## 1.4 Purpose and questions

This study compares the TYMM and IB learner profiles as texts, asking what conception of the person each encodes and what the difference reveals about how educational purpose can be stated in national and transnational policy space.

Three questions guide the analysis:

1. How does each profile construct the ideal learner: through what categories, at what level of specification, and in whose voice?
2. Where do the two profiles correspond, where do they diverge, and what does each decline to name?
3. What do those patterns show about the relationship between a profile's institutional situation and the account of the person it is able to give?

The third question carries the argument. The study's claim is not that the two systems hold different values, which would be unsurprising and largely true. It is that the difference between them registers first in the grammar of the two documents and only afterwards in their content, and that this has been missed because comparisons of learner profiles have generally compared what the profiles say rather than how they are written.

## 1.5 Contribution

The study makes three contributions.

It brings a major new national framework into English-language comparative curriculum scholarship, with its content documented at word level rather than summarised. TYMM's ten characteristics are specified by 61 component definitions, and no existing English-language treatment works from them.

It shifts the analytic register of learner profile research from content to form. The findings reported below turn on grammatical person, on whether collectives are named, and on the presence or absence of qualifying clauses. These are properties of the text that a thematic comparison passes over, and they distinguish the two profiles more sharply than any inventory of values does.

And it offers a procedure for comparing a national with a transnational framework without treating either as the baseline: the first coding cycle is built from the national text's own conceptual apparatus, and the interpretive consequences of that choice are reported rather than concealed. Whether the procedure generalises is an open question, but the alternative, importing a transnational framework's categories and calling the result a comparison, has a known failure mode.
